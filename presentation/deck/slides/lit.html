The gap we fill

No study we found holds payoffs fixed and varies only the emotion the counterpart expresses, across Ultimatum, Dictator and Trust roles, with manipulation checks and pre-registered replications.

KRISHNA · 0:45 — We built on two literatures. In people, the Ultimatum and Trust games show that unfair offers get rejected and strangers get trusted; the responder’s anger predicts rejection, the insula responds to unfair offers, and Knoch showed that disrupting the right DLPFC makes people accept unfair offers they still call unfair. Van Kleef showed that expressed anger is read as information about the other person. On the AI side, Horton, Aher and Mei showed that LLMs can stand in for economic agents, and the closest work, Mozikov and colleagues, puts the emotion into the model itself. The gap: nobody had varied only the emotion the other player expresses, at fixed payoffs, across these three games, with pre-registered replications.
