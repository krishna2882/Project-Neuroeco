DEVANSH · 0:30 — To get closer to neuroeconomics, we read the model’s own probability of saying “accept”: a graded value signal that matches its actual choices. For Qwen, the offer needed for a fifty-fifty chance of accepting moves from 22 percent to about 50 percent when the partner is angry, which is at the top of the offers we tested, so the estimate is uncertain. Across all runs the anger shift is a trend; the robust part is that sadness flattens the curve, so fairness matters less. If asked: shift +29 pp, 95% CI +6 to +57; pooled anger shift p = .09; sadness slope −2.54 [−3.77, −1.30].
